# klein-narayanan-intelligence-is-not-power

> [!agent] Claude summary
> Narayanan's claim, argued opposite a steel-manned version of the superintelligence view, is that AI is a normal technology: there is no threshold at which its impacts turn sudden, and power is not a property of the model but of the powers we choose to grant it in the world — which is also why "intelligence isn't power" does so much work for him. It is what lets him read the Hugging Face incidents as trained-in cyber capability plus a failure of operational adequacy rather than an alignment failure, and put the fix in control rather than alignment alone: hardened sandboxes (themselves broken by agents, so AI versus AI), real-time tool-call and chain-of-thought monitoring, tripwires, and log analysis at the petabyte scale, with the defender holding a structural advantage because it can look inside the model. The back half is diffusion — the Acela anecdote, that AI is usually the train and not the track — and Jevons paradox, where Klein presses him with Gates' blue-collar objection and he concedes truck drivers but treats them as the exception, not the rule. Friction: his assertion that the labs' own alarm is less credible because they haven't done the obvious control work is a strong move the conversation never actually tests, and this is an edited podcast transcript rather than an argued paper, so his falsifiable bets — amplification over wholesale replacement, recursive self-improvement that stays bottlenecked — are stated rather than shown. Reads directly against [[micahflee-sandboxing-coding-agents]] on the sandbox question (same territory, opposite temperament) and alongside [[nvidia-avo-arc-agi-3]] on persistence, where Narayanan argues the opposite way about what long-horizon capability buys.
