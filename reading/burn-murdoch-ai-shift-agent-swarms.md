# burn-murdoch-ai-shift-agent-swarms

> [!agent] Claude summary
> An edition of the FT's "The AI Shift" newsletter where data journalists Clara Murray and John Burn-Murdoch compare agent-swarm habits, with Murray's week-long Codex test as the centerpiece: two real data projects, dozens of parallel agents, and by Friday a price dataset with silent systematic errors and a calculator she couldn't stand behind. Her strongest point is that agents invert the usual trade — building gets dramatically faster, but the verification load lands on her, and overseeing bots is a bad way to actually understand data. Burn-Murdoch describes the practices that make the load survivable: "adversarial audit" (a second model told to assume errors), audit trails with a source URL per data point, replicating known results before starting, rewriting startling analyses from scratch by hand, and a hard line reserving titles, colours and prose as human-only. His headline claim is his "personal Jevons paradox": full-day verification hasn't shrunk his ambition for what he takes on, it raised the floor of what's feasible. The frame comes from Arvind Narayanan — see [[klein-narayanan-intelligence-is-not-power]] for his decide-execute-deliver split, which maps exactly onto Burn-Murdoch's chart workflow, not judging by whether agents get used but by which part of the work you hand over.
